> A <span class=definition>Mathematical Statement</span> is a **<span class="emphasis">sentence</span>** that can be either true or false (but not both).

They can be in the forms of axioms, definitions, lemmas, theorems, etc.

Theorems and lemmas (and axioms?) are "properties" as we learned in Grade 12
# Types of statements
### <span class="definition">Theorem</span>

> A difficult to prove (but proven!) statement. Far more involved than an lemma

### <span class="definition">Lemma</span> 

> A smaller provable true statement. Usually used to prove theorems
### <span class="definition">Axiom</span> 

> A statement (so also true or false) that we assume is true "because it is obvious". 
> In MATA22, we treat them also as definitions.
> 
>One example of an axiom is [[Supremum and Infinium (and max and min)#Least Upper Bound Property (Completeness axiom)|the least upper bound property]].

### <span class="definition">Corollary </span> 

> A statement that can be based based on a previously proven theorem

### <span class="definition">Definition</span> 

Look [[Definitions|here]] for more info

# Structure of statements

> A "," in a mathematical statement is the same as saying "such that" or s.t.
> 
> Note that it suggests the part "after it" is true. For instance, if we have the statement $$\forall x \in D, f(d)$$ that means we are suggesting that for every value of x, f(d) is true (it *exists*). 
> 
>  
> Also remember that *this* "such that" is different from set builder notation's "such that" (: or |). Do not mix them up!!!!
