Here are the 2 quantifiers we know

| Symbol    | How to read it   | Usage                                                        | Meaning                                                           |
| --------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $\forall$ | For **a**ll      | $\forall x, P(x)$                                            | For all x, P(x) is true                                           |
| $\exists$ | There **e**xists | $\exists x, P(x)$<br>or<br>$\exists x\ such\ that\ P(x)$<br> | "There exists at **least one**" value of x such that P(x) is true |
>[!note] The order of quantifiers can change the statement
> 1. $\exists y \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ such that } \forall x \in \mathbb{Z}, y > x$ 
VS.
>2. $\forall x \in \mathbb{Z}, \exists y \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ such that } y > x$
>
>1 is false, since there is no "greatest" number than Y could be
>2 is true, and we can prove it!
>
> > [!example] Proof. (Referencing Week 2b recording at 15:00)
> >Let $x \in \mathbb{Z}$
> >Choose y = x + 1
> >Then $y\in\mathbb{Z}$ by properties of numbers
