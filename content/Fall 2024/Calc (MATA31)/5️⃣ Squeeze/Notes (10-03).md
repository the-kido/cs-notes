<span class="definition">The Squeeze Theorem</span>

Let L, c \in \mathbb{R} and suppose d > 0 such that functions f, g, h are defined on a d-punctured interval around c.

The squeeze theorem contains two conditions!
If
1. $g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x)$
2. $\lim\limits_{x \to c} g(x) = \lim\limits_{x \to c} h(x) = L$
Then
 $\lim\limits_{x \to c} f(x) = L$

The proof is actually pretty easy. The only thing to note is the intuition as to why delta is the min of delta1, delta2, and *d*. Also, the choice of epsilon can be anything <= epsilon, then using |\*| properties everything magically works out.
