## Accomplishments
>**Always frame your experiences around impactful accomplishments**.

It's better to explain your accomplishments (Results and outcomes) rather than job responsibilities or tasks.

Take it in the form of `(What you did) + (What the positive result was)
`

| Job Task                           | Possible Accomplishments                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Planned events                     | Organized a charity event for 200 people that raised $10,000.                                     |
| Conducted training sessions        | Conducted training sessions for 40 plus student volunteers which received positive feedback.      |
| Developed a form processing engine | Developed a form processing engine that enabled non-technical staff to process web forms quickly. |
And, if you did achieve something, be more specific as to how you did it.

| Vague Accomplishment         | Specific Accomplishment                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Improved Efficiency          | Cut data processing time by 50% by building a new cloud data infrastructure leading to more timely insights.                                        |
| Raised customer satisfaction | Evaluated customer complaints for class project and identified three key service issues and potential resolutions, improving customer satisfaction. |
| Achieved something awesome!  | Developed an app that received 100 downloads in a day.                                                                                              |

## Identifying my own accomplishments
1. Figure out how I am different
	1. What have I done above normal duties?
	2. When was I recognized for excellent work?
	3. How have I improved things? What initiatives?
	4. What made me a great member?
2. Add facts / figures / numbers
	1. As a past supervisor or something for specific metrics
3. Figure out how those benefited the organization

### Figuring out accomplishments
- Everyone has accomplishments (even if they may be small!)
- While the end result itself might not seem very important, sometimes **the process of how we got there** can be a noteworthy accomplishment

- Is not a fixed work or conclusion, but a catalyst for further professional development.

# 2.2 Accomplishment Statements
- Are written in past or present tense 
- Should not contain personal pronouns (ie: I, me, my) or filler words, such as “the”, “a”, “which”, “that” 
- Should be specific, relevant and quantifiable (wherever possible). 
- Are normally formatted as: Skill + Experience + Result (<span class="definition">SER</span>)
	- The "Skill" is an action verb
- Start with strong verbs that sound proactive.

“Exceeded guest experience and provided quick and friendly service by reducing food preparation time by 40%.”

#### Accomplishment Statements for Course Work
##### Tip #1
- Don't focus on knowledge, but rather the skills demonstrated or the application of the knowledge 

Don't say you "learned how to use MATLAB" in a course, say you "used MATLAB in business environments by conducting data models in MATLAB".
##### Tip #2
To highlight an academic or personal project, break down your project into stages and identify what you achieved at each stage

#### Tailoring Accomplishment Statements
We can use the RATS formula instead of the SER formula. (WHY DID WE EVEN LEARN THE SER ONE THEN)
<span class="definition">RATS</span> can be reordered to emphasis the most relevant details.

R-A-TS --> Result, Action, Task / Situation  
- Replace the TS with "What it caused / what it was for". It makes more sense to me

>[!example]
> Improved office efficiency (Result) 
> Developed an Excel database (Action) 
> It cut 4 hours per week off of administrative time (Task/Situation).

# Resumes
Header should include
• Your first and last name 
• Phone number 
• University of Toronto email 
• Address: Some students would prefer not to include their complete home address on their documents; in these cases, list the city and province you live in. 
• Optional components: Your LinkedIn profile link, GitHub link, or any personal websites you believe contain professional and relevant samples of your work.

Fonts like Arial, Times New Roman, Calibri, Verdana, etc. are clean, crisp, easily readable and ATS friendly

Avoid using tables, columns, other fancy formatting, unusual fonts or colored text

### 1. The Education Section
- Include all dates, locations and degrees, diplomas, trainings, and/or certifications received. Also include any relevant academic achievements or certifications.
- After completing B50, do not include high school education.

## 2. The Skills Summary Section
- Its purpose is to pique a recruiter’s interest
- It is either a 
	- Skills Summary: A concise list of your key skills and abilities relevant to the job, OR 
	- Summary of Qualifications: A brief overview of your qualifications and accomplishments that demonstrate your suitability for the position.
- Must be highly <span class="emphasis">tailored</span> and concise
	- Integrate only relevant key words from the job posting, and your respective relevant experiences and skills.
- Each <span class="emphasis">skill statement</span> should be structured with `Level of Skill`, `Skill(s)`, and `Proof / Evidence`
	- "3+ years of experience in a hospital setting" <-- Skill level and Skill
	- "Providing patient support through service as a Sunnybrook Hospital volunteer" <-- Example
### 3. The Experience Section
- In reverse chronological order
- This is where you use accomplishment statements (RATS or SER)
- Date on right, use 3-4 bullet.
- There are many subheadings you can use, like
	- Relevant Experience
	- Work/ Volunteer Experience
	- Relevant Coursework
	- Relevant Projects
	- Extra-Curricular Activities
## Tailoring Resumes

- Considered the relevance of each item within your document according to the needs of the role or values of the employer
