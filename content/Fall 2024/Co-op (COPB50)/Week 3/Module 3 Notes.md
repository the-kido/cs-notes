# Cover letters
- Should be tailored
- Lets you add your personal flare and unique thoughts, via highlighting a few key experiences of which you are most proud, because you: conquered a challenge, were recognized for your ability, had a great impact, or just produced something great.
- It displays your skills, talents, and why you’re applying for a position at their company
- The goal is to show your passion for the position and what you do. (So company research is a must!)
- Does not include *everything* about you, just your strongest selling points


>[!note] Make sure to include honorifics and post-nominals!
>**Post-nominals:** the letters after a name that indicate one's accreditations (i.e.. MBA, PhD, PMP), or **Honorifics:** the titles a person may have (i.e.. Professor, Doctor, etc.).
>
>If these are included on the job posting, then include them in the cover letter.

#### Names
- If the addressee does not include their name, then just use their position name 
	- "Dear Data Analyst Hiring Manager" (specific to the job!)
- You are not expected to know the name if they don't include it

## Introductory Paragraph
Should include:
1. Role/company, intro statement
	- This is your first opportunity to demonstrate alignment with employer needs and values. This paragraph should be informed by company research, should specify:
		- The role you are applying for
		- The organization name
		- Where you saw the posting advertised
		- What motivated you to apply for the role.
2. Interest statement
	- Should explain:
		- What inspired you to apply 
		- How your skill set, personality, and experience align to the role
	- You can start with a hook (1 to 2 lines)
		- It's a personal story / anecdote that is relevant to the position and grabs your reader’s attention.
		- Companies like when you show a strong understanding and genuine connection with their brand. 
		- Identifying how the company speaks to you and showcasing evidence of company research is a compelling way to demonstrate your sincere interest in the role/organization. 
3. Thesis statement
	- The conclusion: 1 clear and concise sentence that identifies 2-3 skills (in the job posting) that you'll elaborate on in the body paragraphs (in *story* format)

## Body Paragraphs
- Usually 2 body paragraphs 
- Each paragraph
	- Focuses on a key skill or experience that was presented in your thesis statement 
	- Uses the STARR story format to highlight instances where you excelled in your work 
	- Should relate back to specific tasks that are mentioned in the job posting.

## Concluding Paragraph
They suggest this form:
Remind
- Remind the reader of your interest and passion for the role.
Re-state
- Re-state your key skills and why you’re the ideal candidate for the job (i.e. your thesis, ideally with slightly different wording).
Thank
- Thank the reader for their time & consideration.
Call to Action
- Include a call to action (“asking for an interview”)
Sign off
- Sign off professionally 
	- Sincerely
	- Regards
	- Yours truly
	- Respectfully
- Avoid informal closings 
	- Take care
	- Warm regards
	- Have a great day


# STARR stories
- It's an expanded version of SER or RATS from [[Module 1 notes]]
- They demonstrate that you can generate Results through using your Skills
- Should not exceed 7-8 lines

##### Situation
- State the specific situation where you used __ skills
- Could also be the "role" you had
- Can be merged with "task" to be 1-2 sentences
##### Task
- State the specific task you undertook / role you played
- Your "Responsibilities" for the above role
- It is important to define this in such a way that the Result is more relevant to the job
##### Action
- 2-3 specific actions you took during the task
	- Don't include steps that aren't relevant
- Specify what skills you used
- "I used my communication skills" does not specify <span class="emphasis">how</span> you used them
##### Result
- State the positive result, outcome, or impact of your actions. 
- If your example is a project in progress, then state the predicted results. 
- If you can, quantify your results. 
- Can be 1 line each.
- Do NOT just say "I developed `x` skill"
	- Focus on the outcome; its' obvious that the skill specified in `Action` lead to that outcome.
##### Relate
- Show how this relates to the position.
- Or, Show the skills you used in this situation would apply to this job posting
- Look at the roles / responsibilities section in the job posting to guide this part

## Choosing stories
- Should be 2-3 years new
- Contains keywords for the job posting
