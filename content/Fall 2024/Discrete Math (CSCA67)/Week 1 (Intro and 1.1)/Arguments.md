> <span class="definition">Valid Deductive Argument</span> If all <span class="emphasis">premises</span> are true, the <span class="emphasis">conclusion</span> must also be true.
> Otherwise it is an *invalid deductive argument* or a *fallacy*

>[!note] "Valid Deductive Argument" is often simplified to "Argument"

Here is an example of an *invalid deductive argument*
>[!quote]
>Either the butler is guilty or the maid is guilty. `premise #1`
>Either the maid is guilty or the cook is guilty.  `premise #1`
>Therefore, either the butler is guilty or the cook is guilty `conclusion`

There is a chance the maid is guilty. The premises are true but the conclusion isn't, so it is invalid deductive reasoning.
