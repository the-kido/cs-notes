Probability
- S is the sample space where all outcomes are equally likely
- E is a event. Events are subsets of S.
- The probability P of E is $P(E) = \frac{|E|}{|S|}$
	- Can be expressed as $P(E) = \sum_{x \in E} P(x)$ since each x is a possible outcome of the event.

> The sum of the probabilities of every outcome occurring is always 1

#### Complements
$P(E) + P(\overline{E}) = 1$ where $\overline{E}$ is the complement of E.

#### Conditional Probability
E and F are two events. Note that F must be *possible* (i.e. P(F) > 0)
$P(E|F) = \frac{P(E \cap F)}{P(F)}$
For instance, we have a game where we flip a coin 3 times. If E means flipping at least 1 tail, and we are certain the first flip is tails, then F = flipping tails first. We see that $P(E \cap F)$ = 3/4 since the first flip is T and we're left with the possibilities of TT, TH, HT, HH.
