<span class="definition">Bound</span> variables
- When a variable doesn't matter when defining a set
- $\{x | x^2 < 25 \}$ and $\{y | y^2 < 25 \}$ are examples. "x" or "y" or any other choice does not matter. 

However, if your variable assumes a value (i.e. $x = f(x) + 5$) or something. You can't just now write $y = f(x) + 5$ 
- X is a <span class="definition">free</span> variable
	- Another example is in $y \in \{1,2,3\}$. y is a free variable because you can't just replace it with "x" willy nilly

So, statements like y ∈ {x | x2 < 9} and y ∈ {w | w 2 < 9} are equivalent, since x and w are bound variables.

Don't mess with free variables, but mess with bound variables
