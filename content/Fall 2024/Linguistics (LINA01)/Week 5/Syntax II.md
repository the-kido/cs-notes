<span class="definition">Constituents</span> / <span class="definition">Phrases</span>: Words / groups of words that form a meaningful unit & perform a syntactic function. 
- They serve a special function in a clause (subject, predicate, direct/indirect object)
- They can be combined to form *other* constituents. 

Those group up into <span class="definition">Clauses</span>

Sentences are not linear.
- They always contain a subject and predicate
	- A subject can be a the doer or experiencer.
		- Typically a Noun Phrase
	- Predicates are the actions done to/ did by the subject
		- Always a verb phrase

### Syntactic trees

| Symbol | Represents           |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| Cl     | Clause               |
| NP     | Noun Phrase          |
| VP     | Verb Phrase          |
| AdjP   | Adjective Phrase     |
| AdvP   | Adverb Phrase        |
| PP     | Prepositional Phrase |


>[!example] What is the subject of "Baking gingerbread cookies is my new Christmas tradition."
>The subject is "baking gingerbread cookies" since it is the object which has now changed to becoming "a Christmas tradition"

>[!example] What is the predicate of "I can often hear the sound of singing around New Year's Eve"
>The predicate (VP) includes everything that is not part of the subject.  
>_I_ is the subject, while the rest of the sentence constitutes the predicate.
>So, it's everything else: "can often hear the sound of singing around New Year's Eve"


## Constituency Tests
1. Replacement Test (Substitution)
2. Stand-Alone Test (Question and Answer)
3. Movement Test (Clefting)

>[!note] Not all tests are appropriate for a string of words!  
>If any one of the tests works, then the string is usually a constituent! Ideally, two tests should be used to make a conclusion. It's important to make this distinction!
>

>>[!quote] the subsuitiion test is VERY LIMITED to AdvP and PP
>
>>[!quote] VP does not \[work] on clefting really. only Q/A and replacement work for VP

#### Replacement / Substitution Test
- If you can replace a constituent with another constituent, then the selected unit forms its own constituent. 
> <span class="emphasis">The quick brown fox</span> jumps over the lazy dog.
> <span class="emphasis">It</span> jumps over the lazy dog.
> 
> We replaced the NP with a pronoun. "Pronouns replace NP's"

Another example:
>[!example] The person</span> behind me was making a lot of noise
>If we go by the logic that pronouns can replace nouns, we'd get:
>> \*The <span class="emphasis">he</span> behind me was making a lot of noise
>
>That doesn't sound right. That's because we didn't replace the whole NP of that sentence:
>> <span class="emphasis">He</span> was making a lot of noise. 
###### Common substitutions
- Pronouns replace NP's
- 'There' replaces some PPs
- The pro-form `do so` (or `did so` in the past) can replace VPs
	- <span class="definition">Pro-forms</span>: A word that substitutes for a phrase

#### The Stand-Alone Test
>[!example] The quick brown fox jumped over _the lazy dog_.
>Apply the stand-alone test:
>
>**Question**: What did the quick brown fox jump over?  
>**Answer**: _The lazy dog_

^ This helped us determine the subject of the sentence!

>[!example] Same as above
>> [!fail]
>> **Question**: *The who jumped over the lazy dog?  
>>**Answer**: _quick brown fox_
>>"quick brown fox" is NOT a constituent
>
> >[!check]
> >**Question**: *The who jumped over the lazy dog?  
> >**Answer**: _The quick brown fox_
>"The quick brown fox" IS a constituent 

^ This helped us determine the predicate of the sentence!

>[!example]  My sister dressed up for the party. 
>Q: Who dressed up for the party? 
>A: My sister.
>
>So, "My sister" is a constituent

### Clefting / Movement Test
- If you can move a constituency inside of a sentence while having it sound normal, then it is a constituency. 
 Clefting involves putting a string of words between <span class="emphasis">It was</span> (or It is) and a <span class="emphasis">that</span>.

>[!example]
> 1. She received \[a beautiful bouquet of flowers].   
> 2. <span class="emphasis">It was</span> \[a beautiful bouquet of flowers] <span class="emphasis">that</span> _she received_.

>[!example]
> 1. Antoine enjoys reading his \[new book].
> 2. It is \[new book] that Antoine enjoys reading
> 
> That doesn't make sense! \[new book] is *not* a constituent
