## Articulatory (phonetic) processes
> The articulatory movements that occur during connected speech.
> They serve two purposes
> - To make speech *more efficient*
> - To make speech *less* efficient but more distinct

##### Coarticulation 
When you pronounce `play`, look at how we pronounce `[pl]`. It's a combination of `[p]` and `[l]` but they are not discretely sounded out. Instead, our tongue moves as 

### 1. Assimilation
> When one phone becomes more similar in sound to a nearby sound with respect to some phonetic feature

#### Nasalization
>[!example] Nasalizing vowels which aren't usually nasalized. 
>For instance, in `tan`
>- Before n is pronounced, our velum opens to prepare for the nasal stop 'n' creates. As such, the æ in t`a`n is nasalized. 
>- To mark this, we use a tilda ~

> The above example is *regressive assimilation* since it goes right to left (right phones affect left phones.).
> 	- "Right affects Left"
> *Progressive assimilation* goes left to right, like in `gone [gɑ̃n]` compared to `got [gɑt]`. You can notice a bit of nasalization in 'gone'
> 	- "Left affects Right"

#### Devoicing / Voicing assimilation
Similar to above, but when vowels or *voiced* constants become voiceless. 
>[!example] Liquids and glides can become devoiced after voiceless stops occur in a stress syllable. 
>- In `Plan`, the `[l]` is usually voiced, but not here! Same with `Trick`. This is called <span class="definition">liquid devoicing</span>. Liquids and glides can become devoiced after voiceless stops that occur in a stressed syllable. 
>	- Remember liquids are the \[l] and \[r] and glides are \[j] (like in `yolk`) and \[w]. You add a little circle below the devoiced liquid `[l̥]` or `[r̥]`
>	- This is regressive assimilation since the right `[p]` affects the left `[l]`
>	- To make this work make sure you don't pronounce the `[l]` while saying `plan` because that's not how you normally say it lol.

> Devoicing and voicing are types of _voicing assimilation_


#### Place Assimilation
>[!example] Try sayin `incredible` out loud
>When paying attention to the position of your tongue during the production of `<n>`, you might notice that you pronounced the cluster `<nc>` as `[ŋk]`. 
#### Flapping/tapping
> Occurs when an alveolar stop (either /t/ or /d/) is produced as a tap/flap `[ɾ]`. It's not an r! It looks very similar though.
> Happens when the alveolar stop happens between two vowels.

 

### 2. Dissimilation 
Opposite of assimilation; neighboring phones become *less similar*.
>[!example] 
> `fifth` /fɪfθs/ as \[fɪfts] and `sixths` /sɪksθs/ as \[sɪksts]

### 3. Deletion
>  When a segment has been _removed_

This has to do with making speech faster!

>[!example]
>For instance, `suppose` /səpówz/ is produced as \[spówz]

`spaghetti` is also an example of deletion apparently... 
### 4. Epenthesis
> Adding phones that don't exist on the phonemic level, but the phonetic level. 

>[!example] Hamster
>Hamster /hæmstər/ is pronounced as as \[hæmpstər], with a \[p] inserted.
>`Warmth` and `Something` are other examples!

### 5. Metathesis
When segments or syllables are reordered (usually to make something easier to say). For instance, _<span class="emphasis">pre</span>scription_ as <span class="emphasis">per</span>scription and <span class="emphasis">ask</span> as <span class="emphasis">aks</span>.

### 6. Aspiration / Voicing Lag
> A brief delay before a puff of air.
> Marked with \[$^h$]

>[!example] pin, top, cope.
>Unaspirated: spin, stop, scope 

cope and scope are obvious but not the other examples.

Aspiration affects voiceless stops at the beginning of a _stressed syllable_ while the voiceless stop is immediately followed by a vowel. Note that in the cases where aspirated stops occur next to liquids, coarticulation tends to affect the voicing of the said liquids (producing liquid devoicing)

### 7 Canadian Raising
There are some words where the diphthongs /aj/ and /aw/ are raised to be pronounced as \[ʌj] and \[ʌw]. The second pair are "higher" than the previous two. 


| Canadian Raising does not apply | Canadian Raising applies<br> |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| rise [rajz]<br>                 | rice [rʌjs] <br>             |
| ride [rajd] <br>                | right [rʌjt]<br>             |
| tribe [trajb] <br>              | tripe [trʌjp] <br>           |
| fly  [flaj] <br>                | flight [flʌjt]               |
| wives [wajvz] <br>              | wife [wʌjf] <br>             |
By sounding these out, you can tell the right column is higher than the left column.
