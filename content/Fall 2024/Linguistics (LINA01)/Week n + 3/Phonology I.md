## Phonemes and Allophones

 _back_ [æ] and _beak_ [i] have two different phonemes. You can tell these are two different words because swapping the phonemes changes the meaning of what you just said.
HOWEVER, with <span class="definition">Allophones</span>, swapping two allophones cannot change the meaning of a word. The differences between them are SUPER subtle. For instance, in _bean_ [bĩn] and _beak_ [bik], the `i` and `ĩ` are two different allophones, but sound **almost** identical. 

### Minimal pairs
> A set of two words that differ by 1 sound. This means they should have the same # of sounds.

For instance, `bat` and `cat` are minimal pairs since only the first consonant are different. Same with beak and back. 


>[!example] Consider this pair of English words: ‘brand’ ‘bland’. Are the sounds ​[r] and ​[l] contrastive in English?
>They are a minimal pair, so [r] and [l] are contrastive!

## Phonetic and Phonemic Representations

<span class="definition">Phonemic Representation</span>shows *phonemes* for a unit (word, morpheme, etc.).
- These use `/ /` backslashes

<span class="definition">Phonetic Representation</span> shows all other small bits of info during *speech* (aspiration, nasalization, devoicing, etc.). 
- These use `[ ]` square brackets.




tl;dr, to know if a sound is contrastive, you use a minimal pair where all the segments are the same but the one you want to compare and you say it. If there is obvious contrast, then it's contrastive. If they aren't, they're allophones, which are very similar but not exactly so and definitely do not change the meaning of a word. 

Phonemic: Phonemes of a word that don't depict how a word is actually said with `\\`
Phonetic: Actually shows how it's said with `[]`
