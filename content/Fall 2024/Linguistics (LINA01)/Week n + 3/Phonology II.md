Sonorant Sounds
- Vowels, glides, liquids, and nasals.

<span class="definition">Maximize Onset Principle</span>
- The Onset should be as long as possible
- There can only be three consonants in the onset if the first onset is /s/

- Onset
- Rhyme
	- Contains the nucleus and coda
- Nucleus
	- Usually a vowel or diphthong
	- Always exists in a syllable
	- "the core"

# Making syllable structures
<span class="definition">Phonotactics</span> The constraints on how sequences of segments (sounds) pattern
Here's one:
- If an onset has 3 consonants, then:
	- the first is `[s]`
	- the second is a voiceless stop `[p], [t], [k]`
	- the third is a liquid or glide `[l], [r], [j], [w]`


### 1. Syllabification
"how to you segment the structures of words like extreme, applaud, or decline?"
- The onset must be as large as possible
- If there is a voice for a syllable to be in the coda or onset, it will be in the onset instead.
So the way to syllabify is:
1. List out all the phones, create the rhyme and find the nucleus
2. Form the onsets
3. Form the codas

The syllables add up to form the word `Wd`

Phonotactics = the rules of sound combinations
