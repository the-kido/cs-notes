### §5. Non-linear (*single*) Equations
No multivar calculus PHEW. Just simple $f:\mathbb R \to\mathbb R$

We want to look for $x^*$ that makes $f(x^*)$ "vanish". Aka the "root" of $f$ (like in highschool)

For single equations, examples for diff between linear/non-linear is 
1. $f(x) = 2x + 7$ is linear #todo why were we doing matrix stuff for linear equations above? Can u solve $f(x) = 2x+7$ with matrices?
	1. $f(x) = 0$ when $x = -7/2$ so it's a root. Grade 10 stuff
2. $f(x) = 2x^2 + 7$ is non-linear (greatest power $> 1$)
	1. $f(x) = 0$ has no *real* solution $x^*$.
3. $f(x) = x - e^{-x}$ is funny cuz highest power of $x$ "is" $1$. But it has the $b^x$ function so it's not a polynomial. Non-linear
	1. Not obvious if there is no solution or many

Algorithms (to solve) are based on the Fixed Point Problem
The idea is that we wanna show $f(x^*) = 0 \Leftrightarrow x^* = g(x^*)$ 
- $x^*$ is the "fixed point" of $g$. In that it goes in and comes out the same
- There are two ways to find this $g$
	- (first form) $g(x) = x - f(x)$... obviously, or
	- (second form) $x - h(x)f(x)$ where $h$ is the "auxiliary" function
- Why do we do this?
	- Well finding roots are hard. We can't just "guess" the root of $f$ 
	- But now that we have a thing where x is on both sides ( #todo I don't see why that's necessary) we can "converge" to the answer via iteration again
	- (exponents are actually "superscripts")
	- $x^{k+1} = g(x^k)$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots$ until convergence, with a starting guess like $x^0 = 0$
		- Then we do $X$ test for fixed point iteration (**FPI**). 
			- Difference between $|x^{k+1} - x^k| < \text{tolerance}$ isn't changing much
			- If the x doesn't change then yeah it's our answer
		- As for $F$ test though.... what is that?
			- Simple, we test that $f(x)$ gets to $0$. It's literally "did we find a root of $f$"
