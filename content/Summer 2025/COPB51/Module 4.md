The first thing the modules go over is safety. Ensuring that the position's description are clear, that you aren't giving money upfront, that your offer seems too good to be true, etc. are good ways to ensure you aren't being juked by a bad actor. When you do get a, hopefully unsuspicious, position, then you the module goes into your next steps. Immediately you contact the co-op office. After that you deal with preparing for your interview: knowing the location, your outfit, and being prepared before hand are pivotal for your success in the interview. The night before the interview, you should further prepare by getting good sleep and reviewing. On the day of, ensure you eat healthy, bring your essentials, and be conscious of others (i.e. you shouldn't use scents and be punctual to mind others time). Right before the interview, check in and make sure you are presentable and professional. 

As for your outfit, it's not too difficult to put one together. The module suggests well-fitting clothing that's wrinkle-free and "sharp looking" when put together looks professional. As for colours, natural ones are usually the most formal. Feeling good in your outfit adds to your confidence! For assistance with really tight budgets, there are resources available too. When you're finished with the interview, you will often be prompted to ask any questions you may have. You always should, as they give you the opportunity to show you're interested, prepared, and have your values aligned for the role. To ask these insightful questions, you will need to research the company (culture, specifies of the role, etc.). Don't ask yes/no questions or questions you'll have to answer to after getting your offer. Instead, ask about your role (what you will do, what can you to to be successful, etc.), about the company (asking for characteristics about the company), and about professional growth

### 4.3 Questions to ask as end of interview
Do u have questions for me? --> Thats when u ask to demonstrate interest and preperation
2-3 thoughtful questions are good
Important to ask because:
1. Shows ur engaged, confident, interested
2. Shows ur prepraed and align with them
3. I mean u ask questions for answers. you might just earn something!
Research a lot and tailor questions of course
Don't ask about benefits or vacation or salary. Also no Yes or No questions (those are too open-ended)

Questions to ask:
- About the role
	- What could I do during my first few weeks to make the biggest impact?
	- What will success look like for the person in this role in the first 90 days?
	- If I were the successful candidate, what resources would you recommend I review to be better prepared for day one?
- About the company
	- What are your company’s plans for the future regarding [specific project/initiative]?
	- How would you describe the company culture?
	- What’s the team dynamic like, and how does this role contribute to the team’s success?
- About growth and development
	- What opportunities for growth and professional development does the company offer?
	- What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now?
