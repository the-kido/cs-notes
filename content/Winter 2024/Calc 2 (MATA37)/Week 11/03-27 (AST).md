>[!defn] Leibniz series / alternating series (pg. 641)
>$$b_1 - b_2 + b_3 - b_4 + \dots = \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n+1}b_n$$ where $b_n > 0$ 
>or $$-b_1 + b_2 - b_3 + \dots$$
>is an alternating series!
>
><span class="hide">basically + - + - alternate!</span>

>[!example] $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n }{\ln(n)}$ is an AS with $b_n = \frac 1 {\ln(2)}$


>[!example] $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\cos(n\pi) }{n!}$
>By the periodicity of $\cos(x)$, that series is equal to $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}$

### Alternating series test (AST) (pg. 642)
>[!thm] Alternating series test
>Given $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n+1}b_n$ where $b_n > 0$
>IF
>1. $b_n \geq b_{n+1}, \forall n \in \mathbb N$ (if $b_n$ is decreasing)
>2. $\lim \limits_{n\to\infty}b_n = 0$ (the sequence vanishes)
>	- You should do this first. If this one fails, well frick$\dots$ but oh wait div test!
>
>THEN
>- $\sum(-1)^{n+1}b_n$ converges
>
>
>>[!warning] This can only prove convergence; if either hypotheses fail, this test is inconclusive.
>
]
