# Partial Fraction Decomposition (chapter 5.4)

Purpose: To rewrite certain proper rational functions into a simpler partial fraction 
- Turn the complex quotient into simpler quotients
- Useful for integrating proper rational functions


>[!example] $f(x) = \frac{5x+11}{(x+3)(x+2)}$. Integrate! 
> this equal is for pfd --> $= \frac{4}{x+3} + \frac{1}{x+2}$ and this is equivalent when differentiated.
> The new fractions on the right side are simpler partial fractions

>Remember that rational functions are functions of the form $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ where $P,Q$ are polynomials are $Q(x) \neq 0$
>A proper rational function has the $deg(P(x)) < deg(Q(x))$.
>

The signal of this technique is if there's a proper rational function and the denominator can factor into irreducible parts.

The 4 steps of PFD
1. Factor the denominator as much as possible
2. Write the function into pfd form
3. We'll do some *linear* algebra to solve for unknown constants
4. Then we can integrate these simpler partial fractions.
	1. The # of partial factors is the same as the number of irreducible factors in the denominator
