>[!proof] IF $\set {a_n}$ is convergent, then $\set {a_nb_n}$ is also convergent
>
>Suppose $\set{a_n}$ converges to, lets say, $a \in R$. (1)
>We also know $\set{b_n}$ converges to $b\in R$ (2)
>
>WTS $\set{a_nb_n}$ converges. The only way to do that is... with the definition.
>$\exists \mathscr l \in \mathbb R, \forall \epsilon > 0, \exists N > 0 \text{ s.t. } \forall n \in \mathbb N, n > N \implies |a_nb_n - \mathscr l| < \epsilon$
>
>Choose $l = ab \in \mathbb R$ (cuz it looks like what we'd prolly get based on limit laws!)
>Let e be arbitrary.
>
>By 1, we have that $\exists N_1 > 0 s.t. \forall n \in N, n > N_1 \implies |a_n - a| < \frac{1}{1 + |b|}\epsilon / 2$
>By 2, we have that $\exists N_2 > 0 s.t. \forall n \in N, n > N_2 \implies |b_n - b| < \frac{1}{1+|a|}\epsilon / 2$
>By 2 (again), we have that $\exists N_2 > 0 s.t. \forall n \in N, n > N_2 \implies |b_n - b| < 1$
>

>Choose $N = \_\_ > 0$
>Suppose n > N.
>THEN
>|a_nb_n - ab| = (we can't factor to use our known into, so we add 0!) = $|a_nb_n - ab_n + ab_n - ab|$
>$=|b_n(a_n - a) + a(b_n - b)|$
>$\leq |b_n(a_n-a)| + |a(b_n-b)|$ by triangle inequality
>$=|b_n||a_n-a| + |a||b_n-b|$ by $|\cdot|$ props
>

>[!note] You cannot take $|a_n-a| < \frac 1 {b_n}$ or replace the epsilon with something that depends on $n$. (FUNDAMENTALLY, $\epsilon$ must be constant and cannot rely on any *variable* or something universally quantified; otherwise $\epsilon$ could be $\epsilon(n)$ or something like that)
