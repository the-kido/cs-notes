We let $\boxplus$ and $\boxdot$ be some generic binary operations.

<span class="definition">Communicative</span> = You can switch the place of the operators. $x \boxplus y = y \boxplus x$
- Division and subtraction are NOT communicative

An operation is communitive if:
$x ⊞ y = y ⊞ x$   $\forall x, y$


<span class="definition">Associative</span> = You can group operands in fancy ways. $x \boxplus (y \boxplus z) = (x \boxplus y) \boxplus z$
- Subtraction is NOT associative. $1 - (2-3) \neq (1-2) - 3$

An operation is  associative if
x ⊞ y ⊞ z = x ⊞ (y ⊞ z)
- the brackets are not required on x and y since we add from left to right. The expressions is equal to ($(x ⊞ y) ⊞ z$) in that regard
