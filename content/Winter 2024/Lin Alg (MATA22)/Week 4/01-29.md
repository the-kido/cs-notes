>[!thm] [Let $S \subseteq V$] S is a basis of V iff every vector in V has a unique representation in span(S)
>
>This implies S is a subset of V.

$\Rightarrow$ (using the first technique)
Suppose S is a basis.
As such, V = span(S). Thus, every vector in V has a representation in S. As S is L.I. (refer to those 3 equivalent expressions from [[01-27]]) , all vectors in span(S) are represented uniquely.
$\Rightarrow$ (using second technique)
Suppose S is a basis and $x \in V$. As $v = span(S)$, $x \in span(S)$. As $S$ is L.I. and $x \in span(S)$, $x$ is represented uniquely. 
Thus, all vectors in V are represented uniquely. 
$\Leftarrow$ Suppose all vectors in V have a unique representation in $span(S)$.
Then $V \subseteq span(S)$ as $S \subseteq V$ and V is a vector space $span(S) \subseteq V$. Thus $v = span(S)$.
