>[!thm] The Linear Independence Extension Lemma
>
>>[!proof]
>$\rightarrow$ Direction
>Assume that S \cup \set{\vec x} is linearly independent.
>WTS x \not \in span(S)
>Assume (for sake of contradiction) that x is in the span S.
>Then \exists a1, \dots a_n \in \mathbb F and v_1, \dots, v_2 \in S s.t. x = a_1v_1 + \dots + a_nv_n 
>=> 0 = (-1)x + a_1v_11 + \dots + a_nv_n
>As -1 != 0 and x is not in  S, we've shown there is a non-trivial solution. SO S\cup{s} is L.D. Therefore we have a contradiction. Thus x cannot be in the span. 
>$\leftarrow$ direction
>Suppose x \in Span(S). For the sake of contradiction, assume S\cup{x} is L.D. There exists a_1, \dots, a_{n+1} \in F and v_1\dots v-N \in S, with at least 1 a_i \neq 0, s.t. a_1v_1 + \dots + a_nv_n + a_{n+1}x = 0.
>Suppose a_{n+1} = 0. Then S is L.D, contradicting out assumption. Thus a_{n+1} \neq 0. Let \bar a be the multiplicative inverse of -a_{n+1}. Then we can show x = (\bar a a_i)v_i + (\bar av_2)v_2 + \dots + (\bar a a_n)v_n.
>Therefore x \in span(S) $\Rightarrow\!\Leftarrow$ (by contradiction).
