### Community Music

<span class="testable">Community Music</span>
- It can be formal/informal. 
- it's very broad. it's an ethos! A practice and way of thinking. it is fundamental; a birth right.
	- The gap to make music is so big now that it feel as though it's not a fundamental thing we can do.
	- So, community music comes from the belief that music-making is fundamental and a "crucible" for expression (social, political, etc.)
	- On the formal side, it's a orchestra where there is still conductor
	- On the informal side, it's friends making a band w/ a leader who organizes it all but still far more casual.
- <span class="testable">Professional Musician</span>
	- A person who earns all/part of their income making music
- <span class="testable">Semi-professional Musician</span>
	- A person who plays music for money sometimes
- <span class="testable">Amateur Musician</span>
	- A person who doesn't get paid to play but still makes music.
- Success in music is usually defined as "full-time processional" or "getting signed" although there are really no full-time jobs in the sector
	- Other aims could be enjoyment, identity, self-expression, sense of community, or appreciation for the craft

<span class="testable">Situated Learning</span> 
- A theory of learning that centers of unique community relationships / learning contexts
	- learning that focuses on relationships and learning contexts
- aka apprenticeship learning
- Experts teach the novices via peripheral participation
	- So a group of nurses will do their thing and when a new nurse joins they will just hang around the nurses and listen/see what they do until they understand what's going on and start being active
	- This is how most people learn informally according to Green
