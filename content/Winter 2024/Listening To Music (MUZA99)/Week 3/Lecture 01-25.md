<span class="emphasis">Sound</span>: The "broadest" term associated with nature/ urban sounds/ sound effects/ other nonmusical and auditory phenomena
<span class="emphasis">Noise</span>: Used usually negatively (noisy sounds aren't usually good)
- Associated with unpleasant or disturbing sounds. Contruction, etc.

<span class="emphasis">Music</span>
- Organized sound. 
- Adheres to some conventions given by a genre and form.
- Usually pleasing, consonant (in agreement), and harmonious sounds.


<span class="testable">Acoustics</span>: The science of the production, propagation, and perception of sound.
<span class="emphasis">Sound is a vibration</span>
- Sound waves are transmitted from the sound through air then to our ears and brain.
<span class="testable">Treble</span>: Higher frequencies
- High-pitched sounds (high voice, trumpet, electronic hums, etc)
<span class="testable">Bass</span>
- (low voice, tuba, rumbles, bass guitar)



- <span class="emphasis">Fequency</span>: The number of vibrations/cycles occurring per time unit (usually seconds as in Hertz)
- <span class="emphasis">Pitch</span>: The interpretation of frequency. "The quality of a sound as a function of its fundamental frequency (i.e. the hertz)"
- <span class="emphasis">Tone</span>: A sound of a definite pitch.
	- Sounds of indefinite pitch would be rain (can be low or high pitch)
- <span class="testable">Timbre</span>: The "colour" of a sound. The character or quality. Tone colour
	- A human singing and a guitar strum of the same note will still be differentiable by timbre.

Comparing timbre
- We think of timbre as tone colour.
	- It's analogous to colour in visual art.
- Words include
	- Dark, bright
	- Warm, cool, cold
	- Shading (dynamic shading)
	- Sound painting
	- Soundscape
- <span class="emphasis">Synaesthesia</span> is a phenomenon where you can hear colors, taste words, etc.

Choosing timbre
- Timbre is chosen to best express the indented meaning. The effectiveness of a timbre is subjective though.
- Back before the Baroque period, music notation rarely specified what instrument was used. You can use any instrument to play music.
- Some instruments have different ranges (i.e. bass guitar doesn't go to high)

Volume/Dynamics

	- <span class="testable">Dynamics</span>: The aspect of music dealing with degrees of "loudness"

	- Forte (loud) (f)
	- Mezzo-forte (mf)
	- Mezzo-piano (mp)

	- Fortissimo (ff)/ Pianissimo (pp)

	- `Forte possibile` (fff)/ `piano possibile` (ppp):

	- Niente (n; nothing)

### Instruments
Classification Systems (there are two we study)
- Why classify instruments? It's useful for producers and consumers

Orchestral Families
- Organized via design, material (brass), means of sound production (wind), and performance techniques.
Hornbostel-Sachs Classification System
- The way that a sound is produced. (plucking a string, for instance)

Here are the 4 traditional orchestral families
1. <span class="testable">Woodwinds (winds)</span>: Aerophones. Have enclosed, vibrating air column set into motion with reeds or just by blowing into an aperture.
	1. With reeds: clarinet; saxophone; oboe; bassoon.
	2. W/o reeds: Flute, recorder, ocarina. wistle
2. <span class="testable">Brass </span>: Aerophones. Usually made with brass. Buzzing of lips (vibrating air column)
3. <span class="testable">Percussion</span>: Idiophones/membranophones. Something that can be struck/moved around.
	1. Pitched: xylophone, glockenspiel
	2. Non-pitched: snare drum, bass drum, cymbals, etc.
4. <span class="testable">Strings</span>: Chordophones in which one or more strings create a vibrating system
	1. Bowed (violin, viola, cello, double bass)
	2. Plucked (guitar, ukulele, etc.)

Then there are 3 other families
1. Voice: Human singing.
	1. Soprano, alto, tenor, bass.
	2. New categories being "high, medium, and low" voice
2. Keyboards. Striking keys on a keyboard.
	1. Piano. Organ.
3. Electronics.
	1. Synth, electric guitar, any electronic instruments basically.

Hornbostel-Sachs Classification System
1. <span class="testable">Aerophone</span>
	1. Column of air is the vibrating system
2. <span class="testable">Idiophone</span>
	1. Instruments that make sound via its primary material.
		1. I.e. a cymbal. 
3. <span class="testable">Membranophone</span>
	1. Where a "membrane" vibrates.
		1. I.e. the animal skin that makes a drum is what vibrates. 
4. <span class="testable">Chordophone</span>: An instrument where sound is made by strings.
	1. Usually these strings are bowed, plucked, or struck.

Woodwinds:
- Construction
	- <span class="testable">Mouthpiece</span>: The part of an instrument placed against the lips. you blow into it to create sound.
	- <span class="testable">Key</span>: The small discs that open and close sound holes on the instrument. (usually metal, like on a flute)
- Techniques:
	- Key clicks (pressing keys without blowing air)
	- Multiphonics (producing multiple notes using some magic)
	- Singing while playing (vocalizing one note (usually humming) while playing a different note)
	- Tonguing....
Brass
- Construction
	- <span class="testable">Mouthpiece</span>: The part where the lips are placed. The person blows into it while buzzing their lips to create a sound.
	- <span class="testable">Valve</span>: A mechanism used to change the length of tubing. The person presses the valves to activate them.
	- <span class="testable">Bell</span>: a flared (like lipped but the opposite way) *end* of an instrument.
- <span class="testable">Mutes</span>: A (usually cone-shaped) plug that is added into the bell to change its timbre. 
	- A plug that literally attempts to mute the brass instrument.
	- A kazoo can be a mute
Percussion

	- <span class="testable">Mallet</span>: A stick with a round head used to hit an instrument. Plastic or wood usually
	- <span class="testable">Drum Set</span>: A set of drums and cymbals. 
		- kick drum, snare drum, hi-hat, crash cymbal, etc.
