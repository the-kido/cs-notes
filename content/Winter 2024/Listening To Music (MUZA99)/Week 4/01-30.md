Music is "temporal" (it exists in "time"), like dance, theatre, comedy, and movies are.
- These disciplines define and structure the viewer's time.
Other art forms exist out of time (i.e. painting, sculptures, etc.).
- These let the viewer to define the viewing structure time themselves

### Influences on Our Experience of Time  
- Venue and performance practice influence our experience of time  
- An example from art ‘in time’: in a classical concert hall, lights are off and movement is  
restricted  
	• Limits the amount of time viewers spend at the show (defining the amount of time)  
	• Limits which durations/ genres/ time periods/ characters of pieces they will hear  
	(structuring their time)
- An example from art ‘out of time’: in an art gallery, lights are on and movement is relatively free (although ‘performance practice’ still applies!)  
	- Viewers have the freedom to enter and exit the gallery at will (defining the amount of time)  
	- Viewers can spend their time viewing whichever artworks they like for as long as they like (structuring their time)

### Tempo
<span class="testable">Tempo</span>: the speed at which music is performed. 
- "The rate per unit of time" in performance, specifically.
- Measured in beats per minute. <span class="testable">"bpm"</span>
Here are the Italian terms that denote tempo in classical music:
- Largo: very slow.
- Adagio: slow
- Adante: medium slow
- Moderato: medium
- Allegro: fast
- Presto: very fast

Tempo Changes
- Accelerando (accel.) (becoming faster)
- Ritardando (rit.) (become slower)
- <span class="testable">rubato</span>: when tempo is flexible at the discretion of the performer. 
	- Literally "in robbed time". rubato = robbing time, yes.
	- There is no "beat" or it's hard to tell.

## Rhythm
Rhythm: "Patterns of movement in time".
They are very abstract. birds, dogs barking, etc. 

Music has different rhythms continuously throughout.

But we like to organize things. Rhythm is used to organize music
<span class="testable">Note</span>: A note is a tone of definite pitch. A sound for a certain amount of time
<span class="testable">Rest</span>: Silence for a certain amount of time

### Phrases
<span class="testable">Phrases</span>: A group of notes forming a distinct unit. 
- Slurs notate phrases (the big curves)
- Phrases can be different lengths? 
	- The song example w/ alicia keys is pretty clear w/ this
	- the first phrase repeats 4 times. the one after is longer

<span class="testable">Swing Rhythm </span>
a rhythmic feel in which eighth notes are intentionally played unevenly to create both a feeling of ease and a sense of tension in the music
- my version: When one of the ending notes in a bar are played out of sync.
- If you pretend to be a metronome and keep a consistent beat, you'll notice the rhythm doesn't follow that beat!

### Polyrhythm and Arhythmic 
- Polyrhythm: 2+ rhythms sounding simultaneously.
- <span class="testable">Arhythmic</span>: music which lacks any clearly discernible rhythms.

# Metre

<span class="testable">Metre</span>: the pattern in which a steady succession of rhythmic pulses is organized
- Usually groups of 2, 3, 4, and 5
<span class="testable">Time Signature</span>: The sign placed at the beginning of a piece of music to indicate its metre
- It's also called the "metre" on paper so that's confusing yep.
- The top number is the number of beats per bar
- the bottom number is the value of each beat (i.e. each beat is a forth, eight, etc.)

#### Bars and Downbeat
<span class="testable">Bar</span>: A unit of musical time. Has a fixed # of notes (depends on the metre). Seperated w/ bar lines
<span class="testable">Downbeat</span>: The first beat of in a bar 
- It's accented a bit. That's why there's a difference between
- <span class="testable">Accent</span>: Emphasis or stress on a note. Kinda intuitive.

#### The "bottom" # of the time signature
- It's complicated. 
- It just shows the subdivisions of one note. 4 means the beat has 4 quarters. 8 means the beat has each note as eights.

#### Misc things
- <span class="testable">Upbeats</span>: One or several notes that happen before the downbeat. 
	- A.K.A the "pickup"
	- In happy birthday, the upbeats are "hap-py" and the downbeat is on "birth"-day.
- <span class="testable">Backbeat</span>: A stark attack on beats 2 and 4 of a 4-beat measure (i.e. 4/4)
	- Almost always in the snare drum.
	- Repeat `bum-tss-k-tss` (the `tss` is the snare drum; the back beat)
- <span class="testable">Syncopation</span>
	- When we do something on an offbeat or weak beat.
	- Ragtime and jazz. Really in jazz tho.
	- Doing things in the in-between of beats also.
