### Case study: Music in Aerobics Class
- more de-nora stuff
- <span class="testable">Agency</span>: "A capacity for emotional, embodied, cognitive being"
	- i think the capacity to **act and make decisions**
- For instance, in the gym we can use music to sustain ourselves over a 45 minute session.
- Aerobic order could be motivation, whereas disorder could be exhaustion or distraction.
- There are 3 involved parties
	- The music production companies making music for demand
	- Instructors who use specific music to boost aerobic order by making playlists
	- Class members who use said musical materials and focus on the elements that keep them going (ignoring irrelevant or confusing things like syncopation...)
- So in that example the class members are active sense-makers appropriating the music for their purposes (they are not just "passive recipients").
	- Ig that's what she means by agency
- So in all, music structures the behavior of people!

Primary signification --> The "internal references and relationships" 
- 1 chord going to another chord has a relationship; how the meter relates to the tempo, etc.
- This are all inherent to the music
Secondary signification --> The connotations of the music
- I.e. a song you listen to that you relate to something in your life, or a feeling.
- it effects the "pace, force, or style of movement"

Back to the aerobics example:
- Music is composed for different sections of a session (i.e. warmup, pre-core, core, cool-down, floor exercises,)
- Music fits the "aerobic grammar" of the moment.
- Other sonic / musical features that are curated for specific settings could be
	- The volume 
	- Synthetic sounds (hyperreal clarity?)
	- Rhythm in the foreground; vocals in the background
	- etc.

## Music for Visual Media
- video games!!
Media: Categories of art (film, dance, etc.) as a means of communication (e.g. through "the media")
<span class="testable">Audiovisual (AV)</span> media uses both audio (sound) and visual (sight) elements. Most visual media is audiovisual. 

Visual media includes:
- Electronic Media (films, tv shows, social media videos, video games)
- Live Media (dance, circus, theatre, etc.)

In visual media, music sets a tone usually.
<span class="testable">Mickey Mousing</span>: Where the music closely aligns with what's going on in the screen
- Think "tip toeing" playing "pizzicato"
- Soundtrack dissonance: when music contradicts a scene (often in an ironic way) . Think of horror movies and nursery rhymes.
 Music can also reveal details that aren't said or shown.

### Program Music
- <span class="testable">Program Music</span>: Music that represents something outside of itself
	- Also called programmatic music
	- I.e. violin representing bird calls for a song about spring; descending piano for thunder in the summer
- <span class="testable">Film Score</span>: Music that accompanies a film.
	- Specifically, it's program music!
	- To score means to make a score for a film.
	- #todo why is film scores a type of program music 
- <span class="testable">Main Title Theme</span>: Music heard during the opening sequence and/or title sequence of media.
	- Leitmotifs often occur within them.
- <span class="testable">Leitmotif</span>: A short phrase that recurs over the course of a work of visual media (usually in connection with a character or other thing).
	- Literally "leading motive"
- <span class="testable">Underscores</span>: music that happens under the action.
	- The characters do not hear it. It only plays for us. 
	- In other scenarios it is called incidental music (theatre, dance, etc.)
	- Think of chemical plant 
- <span class="testable">Source Music</span>: The opposite underscore.
	- I.e. a radio playing in the movie that the characters are listening to.
