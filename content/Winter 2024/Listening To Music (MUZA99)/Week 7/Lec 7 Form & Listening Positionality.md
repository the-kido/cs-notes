# Form
<span class="emphasis">Form</span>
- The structure of a song!
- For listeners, form lets us make sense of sonic material (it organizes the sound further)
- For performers, it makes it easier to learn a song
- For composers,  it helps combine musical elements via preexisting formal structures.

We talk about form kinda like architecture
1. Structure (structure of a piece of music)
2. Design (sound design)
3.  Material (sound material)

Form is anything familiar, like leaves on a tree or your daily schedule or even slideshows

### Elements of Form

<span class="testable">Theme</span>
- A "musical idea"

<span class="testable">Repetition</span>
- The restatement of a theme / musical idea
- Related to ostinato; repeated sign

<span class="testable">Variation</span>
- Modifying a theme/musical idea after its first apparent
- Related to development, transformation, elaboration, ornamentation

##### The sections in Verse-Chorus / Verse-Refrain / Verse-Hook 

<span class="testable">Verse</span>
- The thing we don't remember form a song cuz they keep changing! But it usually tells the story
<span class="testable">Chorus</span>
- The sing-along part; maybe includes the title of the song. 
- Usually tells the message of a song.
<span class="testable">Refrain</span>
- Same as the chorus but shorted maybe only 1 or two lines
<span class="testable">Hook</span>
- Same as the chorus.
- The part "that hooks people in"
- Unlike lyrics that are rapped, it is sung
<span class="testable">Pre-Chorus</span>
- Builds to a chorus
<span class="testable">Post-Chorus</span>
- Maintains the energy from the chorus
	- When I analyzed KICKBACK it had that at the end. "happy, lucky, konnichiwa baby"
- Usually repeats / chants something.

<span class="testable">Bridge</span>
- It does NOT bridge things together. Instead, it's a contrasting section. 
- Usually it has unique lyrics or music
- Happens once near the end ish before a climax.
- The "breakdown" could also be a bridge

<span class="testable">Instrumental Solo</span>
- Usually guitar.
- Improvised.
- No words either!

### <span class="testable">Strophic Form</span>
Verse Verse Verse Verse Verse Verse Verse 
- The lyrics are different each verse.
- The music is the same 
- Lots of genres use it!

12-bar-blues could be considered strophic. 
- many religious songs have a strophic form (the hanuman chalisa is a great example!)
- I also think a lot of nursery rhymes do this too!

# Listening Positionality
>Dylan Robinson reading

<span class="emphasis">Positionality</span>: A person's "social stating". A person's identity in relation to others. 
- We all consider this un/consciously. 
- We position ourselves constantly. This is all related to sociology 
<span class="emphasis">Listening is guided by positionality</span>: How our identity of ourselves/biases guides our listening!

<span class="testable">Critical Listening Positionality</span>
- Considering our positionality in relation to the sounds we're hearing
- It's important to consider how we're affected by "normative listening orientations"
- Engagement w/ histories, epistemologies, and cosmologies

<span class="testable">Normative Listening</span>
- Reinforces normative listening habits/biases
	- I.e. you may be listening 'correctly' if you can perceive the content of what is heard.
	- For instance, we've been practicing normative listening all the time throughout this course as Mr. Jacobs here has chosen, with his own biases, our listening list based on what he believes is a good "canon". 
- Normative behaviors perpetuated norms / the right behavior based on standards.

<span class="testable">The "tin ear"</span>
- Refers to a supreme court trial
- Refers to non-indigenous Canadians being unable /unwilling to hear indigenous music

#### History things

Oral history: history passed down verbally
- There is some clear distraction between the two (as seen in that court case)

<span class="testable">Listening as palimpsest</span>
- Refers to an ancient *manuscript* that has been washed off and written over
- Used as a metaphor: listening can be a palimpsest. Listening can write back faded history
- The song `Mehcinut` in our listening list could be considered a palimpsest

<span class="testable">Listening w/ three ears</span> (Listening with the heart)
- "We should listen with our ears and our heart".
- We should "feel the history" when we listen

<span class="emphasis">Witnessing</span>
- For Sto:lo people, being a witness means to be a "living book of knowledge"
	- What's interesting is that the words "listening" and "witnessing" are used interchangeably! Means there is a strong relation between the two in their culture
